# Mental Models

The non-metaphor frameworks named in Hohpe's *Platform Strategy* (via its public table of contents). Use these to make decisions the metaphors dramatize.

> **Provenance rule.** Some of these frameworks are *named* by the book but their full contents are **not** public and are **not** reproduced here (the Fab Four members, the seven Cs, the ACED expansion). Where that is the case, the entry says so and instructs the agent to consult the book rather than invent the contents. Never present an invented definition as Hohpe's. See SKILL.md → Source.

## The Fab Four of technology platforms

- **Status:** Named in the book (Part I). **Members and definition not reproduced here.**
- **What the TOC says:** "Technology drives business models."
- **How to use it:** If the user has the book, apply the actual Fab Four to position the platform's business-model role. Otherwise, cite the chapter and flag platform-type/business-model framing as a gap — do **not** invent a typology.
- **Interview input:** Block 1 asks what kind of platform this is in plain terms; it does not gate the interview on an invented classification.

## The 7 "C"s of platform quality

- **Status:** Named in the book (Part IV). **The seven C words and their definitions are not reproduced here.**
- **What the TOC says:** "Platforms might not be forever, but they do have more C's than diamonds."
- **How to use it:** If the user has the book, walk the actual seven Cs and turn each committed one into a stated promise plus its mechanism. Otherwise, cite the chapter and do **not** fabricate the list.
- **Interview input:** Blocks 3, 6, 7 gather raw material (opinions, abstractions, tenancy) that the real seven Cs would organize.

## ACED your strategy

- **Status:** Named in the book as the addendum "I ACED My Strategy" (Part II). **The A-C-E-D expansion is not reproduced here.**
- **What the TOC says:** "Strategy is the difference between making a wish and making it come true."
- **How to use it:** If the user has the book, apply ACED as the final coherence check. Otherwise, use the generic check "is this a plan or a wish?" and cite the chapter — do **not** invent the acronym's meaning.

## Platform Paradox

- **What it is:** Harmonization at the right layer enables innovation rather than restricting it (HTTP as the proof case).
- **Decision it forces:** Which layer to standardize so that constraint below buys freedom above.
- **Interview input:** Block 2 (users/surprise) and Block 3 (opinion). The surprise is what the freed-up layer produces.

## IT platform vs. IT services (operating model)

- **What it is:** Platforms are self-service and opinionated; services are request-and-fulfill. Renaming doesn't convert one into the other.
- **Decision it forces:** Whether interactions are self-service or ticketed — and what must change to move from ticket to self-service.
- **Interview input:** Blocks 4, 9 (basket/salad, team shape). A ticket queue is the tell that you have a service, not a platform.

## Tiering and slicing

- **What the TOC says:** "One size doesn't have to fit all." (Part VI)
- **What it is:** Offer tiers and slices rather than a single monolithic offering, so different users get different levels/segments.
- **Decision it forces:** How to serve advanced and novice users without forking the platform. *(interpretation — verify against the book)*
- **Interview input:** Block 8 (adoption shape).

## The shape of platforms (adoption is not linear)

- **What the TOC says:** "Platform adoption is all but linear." (Part VI)
- **What it is:** Adoption does not grow in a straight line. **The book's specific shape is not reproduced here — do not assert an S-curve or a "chasm" as Hohpe's unless the user confirms it from the book.**
- **Decision it forces:** Which adoption segment you serve now, and what mechanism (not mandate) feeds the next.
- **Interview input:** Block 8. Combine with tiering/slicing and the opinionated stance.

## Platform evolution is a cube

- **What the TOC says:** "Platforms may be flat, but their path isn't." (Part VI)
- **What it is:** Growth is multi-dimensional. **The cube's specific axes are the book's and are not asserted here.**
- **Decision it forces:** Which dimension to grow this period and which to hold. *(interpretation — verify against the book)*
- **Interview input:** Block 8, plus roadmap context. Pairs with tiering/slicing.

## Platform, Inc.

- **What it is:** Running a platform team is like running a company — it has customers, a product, a value proposition, and must earn its budget.
- **Decision it forces:** Whether the team operates with product discipline (customers, metrics, roadmap) or as a cost center.
- **Interview input:** Block 9 (team shape). This reframes the team's success from output to customer outcomes.

## Multi-sided / customer-centric platform team (diamond shape)

- **What it is:** Platform teams face multiple parties (users, sponsors, providers) and need explicit interfaces — facades — to each. Self-service doesn't mean anonymous; the team stays customer-centric.
- **Decision it forces:** Who owns each interface, and how the team stays close to users despite self-service.
- **Interview input:** Block 9. Every unowned interface is a silent failure point.

## How the models compose

- **Foundation:** Fab Four (type) → business model → team shape.
- **The bet:** Platform Paradox (which layer to harmonize) → the innovation that harmonization buys.
- **The quality contract:** 7 Cs → stated promises → mechanisms.
- **The operating model:** platform vs. services → tenancy → Platform, Inc.
- **The growth story:** non-linear adoption → tiering/slicing → evolution cube.
- **The coherence check:** "is this a plan or a wish?" — the book's ACED addendum applies here if you have it; otherwise use the generic check.
