# Presentation Outline

Slide blueprint for a platform pitch or exec deck. Small slide count — the anchor metaphor is what the audience remembers. Fill from interview answers; check against failure modes. Filled by the agent after the interview.

> Applies Hohpe's *Platform Strategy*. See SKILL.md → Source.

---

## Slide 1 — Title / governing claim

One sentence: what the platform is for, stated as a claim, not a topic label.
- Fill from: Q1, Q3.
- Check: F7 (no tool list on the title slide).

## Slide 2 — The anchor metaphor

The named metaphor with a single image. This is the slide the audience will quote back.
- Fill from: chosen metaphor (`../references/metaphor-catalog.md`).
- One metaphor domain only.

## Slide 3 — The paradox (harmonization → innovation)

Why standardizing here buys innovation elsewhere. The thesis of the pitch.
- Fill from: Q1b, Q5b.
- Check: F3 (freedom, not straitjacket).

## Slide 4 — The tension

The trade-off the metaphor makes visible — the advantage *and* the cost. Never an all-green slide.
- Fill from: the metaphor's tension + Q4/Q5.
- Check: honesty; buy-in means accepting trade-offs.

## Slides 5–7 — Mechanisms, not magic

Two to three slides, each pairing a platform promise with the concrete mechanism behind it.
- Fill from: Q4, Q5, Q8, Q10.
- Check: F6 (nothing described as magic).

## Slide 8 — What success looks like

The user surprise that would count as success. Concrete, not a KPI dashboard.
- Fill from: Q3.
- Check: F1 (outcome, not mandated usage).

## Slide 9 — Punchline

The sticky one-line takeaway. Often an assertion with an inversion (e.g., "IT platform and IT services are antonyms").
- Fill from: the anchor metaphor + governing claim.

## Slide 10 — The ask / next step

What changes for the audience: the decision, budget, or first users you need.
- Fill from: Q7, Q11.
- Check: F1 (asking for pull/sponsorship, not a usage decree).

## Appendix — Tools and tech

Backstage, ArgoCD, Vault, etc. live here only.
- Check: F7 (never in the opening).
